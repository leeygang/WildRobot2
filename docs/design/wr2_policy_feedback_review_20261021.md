# WR2 rapid foot taps: GPU confirmation and policy feedback — 2026-10-21

## Conclusion

**The current WR2 policy sustains a fast oscillation through its past-action
inputs.** This is the strongest identified mechanism behind the rapid right-foot
taps. The training GPU reproduces the CPU recording. The same oscillation can
also be generated by the saved actor without stepping a physics simulation.

This explains how the current policy sustains its command cadence. It does
not establish why training selected that motion. The
[acquisition root-cause audit](wr2_tb_acquisition_root_review_20261021.md)
finds that fast requests already exist at 20M, before the tested isolated
past-action recurrence becomes autonomous. It also corrects the interpretation
of the physical interventions. The CAPS proposal is deferred; training recipe
changes require an initiating cause supported by evidence.

## 1. GPU confirmation and TB Paper comparison

GPU artifact: `stance_diagnostic_gpu`, source `4055baa`, checkpoint SHA-256
`d1a4eca667db1d80de327768231b3c4a0a38124fa3981570168491160aee83cb`.

Compare deterministic nominal forward +.10 TB m/s recordings, seed 22102,
after 1.5 TB seconds. Contact uses the native floor-force threshold above 1 N.
WR2 times divide by `s=.8240547565`, distances by `s²`, velocities by `s`.
TB uses `s=1`. There are no action interventions in this table.

| Right-foot measure | WR2 GPU | WR2 CPU | TB Paper GPU |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Contact onsets | 93 | 93 | 27 |
| Median loaded interval, TB seconds | .04854 | .04854 | .340 |
| Median interval between contact onsets, TB seconds | .1942 | .1942 | .700 |
| Median forward collider-center advance, TB mm | 3.126 | 2.949 | 63.525 |

| Right ankle request dominant frequency, physical Hz | 6.102 | 6.102 | approximately 1.4 |
| WR2 request dominant frequency, TB Hz | 5.029 | 5.029 | — |

Foot-center advance tracks the same point on the same foot at successive
contact onsets. It is not a qualified stride count. Cached contact-time
geometry is used rather than forwarding integrated qpos to another instant.
Loaded intervals are inferred from consecutive 50 Hz contact labels; support
changes inside a control tick are not resolved by this statistic.

The right foot is unloaded about 76% of the steady WR2 recording. Contacts
usually last two 20 ms control ticks. Net forward speed over the full GPU
recording is **.02010 TB m/s**, against command **.10**; it also drifts sideways
at **+.01090 TB m/s**. Survival and contact avoidance therefore do not establish
useful commanded walking.

All six nonzero fixed-command WR2 GPU recordings have right-ankle request
peaks at **6.04–6.17 physical Hz**. Standing has sustained double support rather
than the fast walking mode. Commands at .05 have zero air-time eligibility
under the native deadzone, yet retain the oscillation: this is a learned mode
that generalizes between walking commands, not a direct instantaneous air-time
command.

## 2. Causal interventions with the frozen WR2 actor

Same selected parameters, forward command, nominal reset, controller and
rewards. Input interventions start after a common 1.5 TB-second warmup and
modify all 15 actor-history frames. Other observations and the native history
update continue normally. Each feature group is held at a recorded temporal
mean; phase is held at a valid sine/cosine pair. These are inference diagnostics,
not training modifications.

The table uses a later 2.5 TB-second analysis start to exclude the intervention
transient. All cases survive the approximately 20 TB-second horizon.

| Intervention | Right ankle request frequency, physical Hz | Median right load, TB s | Full-recording net forward speed, TB m/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Native actor inputs | 6.11 | .049 | .0200 |
| Hold phase | 6.11 | .024 | .00982 |
| Hold past actions | **1.67** | **.218** | .00577 |
| Halve past-action variation | 6.11 | .024 | .00551 |
| Hold joint velocities | 6.11 | .049 | .0179 |
| Hold joint positions | 6.18 | .049 | .0191 |
| Hold gyro | 6.11 | .049 | .0209 |
| Hold torso Euler angles | 6.11 | .049 | .0202 |

Holding past actions removes the fast command loop. It does **not** produce
useful walking: placements become tiny/backward and forward speed worsens.
Permanent removal of the action-history input is not an established correction.
TB uses that input too, to support control with delayed actions.

### Physics and numerical controls

The following independently preserve a request peak near **6.11–6.18 Hz**:

- Solver iterations **10→100**: original duration/contact statistics essentially
  unchanged; max qpos difference from the native CPU control is recorded.
- Physics timestep **5→2.5 ms**, frames **4→8**, preserving the 20 ms control and
  reference/reward clock: right load median .049 TB seconds, net forward .01874.
- Active-joint armature **×.5 /×2**, with native `mj_setConst` recomputed before
  MJX conversion: request frequency persists while achieved amplitude changes.
- Active proportional gains **×.5 /×2**: request frequency persists. Double
  gain increases net forward speed to .05113, but still gives rapid taps and
  lower total return. It is a sensitivity test, not a hardware gain calibration.
- Remove joint friction loss: request frequency persists. This changes actuator
  drag, not floor sliding friction.
- Use native convex CAD foot-part/floor contacts, preserving boxes for foot
  self-contact and summing the actual CAD-floor forces for support/rewards:
  request 6.11 Hz, load median .049 TB seconds, net forward .01864. Mass,
  inertia, armature, damping, joint friction and keyframe positions are checked
  unchanged. Large coplanar mesh faces trigger a compiler warning, so this is
  a geometry counterfactual rather than a proposed production collider.

These controls show that the tested settings do not remove the already
learned command cadence. They do not exclude the original physical model or
geometry as influences on acquisition: the policy weights remain fixed.
The [earlier CAD/floor clearance finding](wr2_foot_plant_review_20261020.md#3-why-the-hovering-appearance-can-coexist-with-reported-contact)
remains valid as a separate visual/contact approximation.

The native controllers also differ: TB clips both torque directions with the
same speed-dependent limit; WR2 uses separate acceleration/braking limits.
Its inertia, damping and speed envelope differ as documented in the
[actuator review](wr2_actuator_gap_review_20261018.md). Those differences remain
relevant to execution and acquisition. An actor reproducing its learned
oscillation without a controller does not exclude a controller influence
during training. The WR2 controller docstring now names
its own model instead of attributing its asymmetric envelope to TB.

## 3. Reproduce the oscillation without physics

The recorded native WR2 actor observation contains `a[t-3]` in its newest
past-action field, followed by `a[t-4]` through `a[t-17]` in the older frames.
This lag is verified exactly against saved actions. Native TB has the same
three-tick lag; it is not a newly found WR2 adapter error.

Feed the actor its own previous outputs at that exact lag while holding all
other inputs at a recorded walking context. No environment is constructed and
no solver, contact, force or actuator integration occurs. At two tested steady
contexts, the WR2 **300M** policy generates **6.12–6.22 Hz** requests, with right
ankle-pitch standard deviation **.119–.130 rad**. Holding past-action inputs
instead removes the autonomous oscillation. A third, earlier context converges
to a constant action, so the behavior depends on state/context.

Small-signal linearization around constant-action fixed points confirms the
mechanism. Values below one damp disturbances; values above one amplify them.
The state is the last 17 vectors of 12 policy actions. These are local tests,
not a global stability guarantee or whole-body closed-loop eigenvalues.

| Policy | Past-action feedback spectral radius, two native steady contexts |
| --- | ---: |
| WR2 20M | .960 / .967 |
| WR2 40M | .936 / .932 |
| WR2 140M | .980 / .973 |
| WR2 240M | **1.048 /1.004** |
| WR2 300M | **1.073 /1.021** |
| TB Paper 200M | **.976 /.977** |

The unstable WR2 300M modes predict **6.16 /6.36 Hz**, near the measured
oscillation. Fixed-point residuals are below `2e−7`. WR2 checkpoint tests use
each checkpoint's own CPU walking observations; the initial network's separately
recorded comparison uses 300M contexts and is not a native initial-state test.

The native TB Paper actor's autonomous feedback converges at all three tested
CPU contexts. With the phase clock advancing, its requests follow **1.38 Hz**;
with phase frozen, they converge to constant actions. Thus identical categories
of inputs can support stable, clock-driven walking in TB and an autonomous
fast loop in the current WR2 policy. This is a learned-policy difference, not
proof that the common observation design or Brax PPO trainer is incorrect.

### TB source and CPU limitation

Read local **`paper-walking-baseline`**, revision `a4f693d`, via `git show`.
The frozen local diagnostic checkout's environment, walking reward, controller
and ZMP files match that branch; all model/reference hashes match the saved
Paper GPU provenance. TB policy SHA-256 is
`c3c78fb48b617d974568c1d420e3c5af05d466cf8afee5b8c0c41d475e6b2f46`.

The supplemental TB CPU simulation uses native Brax **.10.5**, JAX **.6.2**,
MuJoCo/MJX **3.2.4**, Flax **.10.6**, NumPy **1.26.4**, Python **3.10.19**.
Its two cases agree exactly before the input intervention at tick 75. The CPU
baseline has .34-second right contacts and a 1.37 Hz request peak; holding
past actions retains approximately that frequency and worsens net forward
speed, **.0882→.0468 m/s**.

**It does not reproduce the GPU pointwise.** First-tick max qpos difference
is `2.54e−5`; first-100-tick max is `.0316`, full-horizon max `.202`
(mixed translational/angular coordinates). The 100-tick agreement assertion
failed and is recorded in the audit. These supplemental CPU results describe
native-source counterfactual behavior, not a new GPU parity score. The main
planting comparison uses the copied TB GPU artifact. TB pure-policy tests use
those supplemental CPU contexts; they need not reproduce a particular GPU
trajectory to characterize the actor at the tested inputs.

WR2 supplemental tests use CPU Brax .14.2, JAX .6.2, MuJoCo/MJX 3.14.0, Flax
.11.2 and high matmul precision. Its copied training/GPU baseline matches the
planting pattern closely, as shown above. The active TB checkout is untouched.

## 4. Why the current objective can retain this behavior

Rescore native and past-action-held WR2 trajectories with the **same** current
weights and controller. They both survive the full horizon.

| Episode return component | Native fast loop | Past-action-held intervention |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Air time | **163.40** | 118.00 |
| XY tracking | 39.88 | 32.05 |
| Contact/reference matches | 16.94 | 21.18 |
| Angular XY | 24.15 | 31.34 |
| Angular Z | 78.66 | 82.09 |
| Leg action rate + acceleration | **−.1846** | −.0078 |
| Total, all terms | **367.31** | 328.23 |

The fast loop earns **39.08** more total return. Its air-time advantage is
**45.40**, while its additional smoothness cost is only **.177**. A softer,
slow contact strategy is not necessarily preferred by the existing objective.
However, this intervention also tracks worse: it is not an example of a good
walking policy being rejected by the objective.

Both TB and WR2 use air coefficient **500**, smoothness coefficients **.05**,
and air credit paid when contact returns. Frequent contact alone does not
multiply air credit; accumulated unloaded duration determines the amount.
The native rule requires no sustained loading or useful placement. TB's
successful copied forward trace also earns substantial air return (**169.40**,
versus WR2 **163.40**) over the nominal horizons. These raw returns have their
native clocks; they are not comparable policy quality scores across models.
Therefore this is evidence of a local objective tradeoff in WR2, not evidence
that the common TB air-time term is intrinsically wrong.

## 5. Training modification deferred

The prior CAPS temporal-regularization proposal is deferred. It targets a
confirmed mechanism of the learned policy, but the initiating cause of WR2's
training divergence from TB is unresolved. The 140M policy already makes fast
requests and has poor forward response despite locally damped isolated
past-action feedback. It is not a demonstrated healthy walking starting point.

Follow the [acquisition audit](wr2_tb_acquisition_root_review_20261021.md)
before selecting a recipe correction. Current training code and parameters
have not been changed by these reviews.
